# 10 — Content & Copy Guide

## Voice

Write the way a good contractor talks to a homeowner standing in the driveway: direct, unhurried, competent, and willing to tell you something you don't want to hear.

**Sounds like:**
> "You've got two real options. Screw-down panel is going to be about half the price and it'll do fine on a barn. If this is the house you're staying in, I'd put standing seam on it — there's nothing going through the surface to leak in twenty years. Either way I'll write you a number for both."

**Doesn't sound like:**
> "Metal Master Roofing is your premier provider of comprehensive roofing solutions, leveraging industry-leading expertise to deliver unparalleled quality and customer satisfaction."

## Rules

1. **Second person.** "Your roof," not "the customer's roof."
2. **Short sentences.** Average under 20 words. Vary the rhythm so it doesn't read like a list.
3. **8th–10th grade reading level.** Not because the audience isn't smart — because they're reading on a phone with a leak in the ceiling.
4. **Concrete over abstract.** "Forty to seventy years" beats "long-lasting." "Two guys and a day and a half" beats "efficient service."
5. **Say the true thing even when it costs a sale.** "If you're selling in two years, don't put a metal roof on it — you won't get it back." That single sentence builds more trust than a page of superlatives, and it makes everything else on the site more believable.
6. **Local specifics, everywhere.** Roads, ridges, weather, the way the wind comes across. This is the one thing a national competitor structurally cannot fake.
7. **Never invent a credential, a year, a review, an award, or a number.** If it isn't confirmed in `01-business-facts.md`, it doesn't get written.

## Banned phrases

`premier` · `state-of-the-art` · `industry-leading` · `unparalleled` · `we pride ourselves on` · `customer satisfaction is our top priority` · `look no further` · `nestled in the heart of` · `in today's fast-paced world` · `elevate your home` · `dream roof` · `top-notch` · `second to none` · `one-stop shop` · `hassle-free` · `we've got you covered` · `at the end of the day`

Also banned: exclamation points in body copy (one per page maximum, in a CTA at most), and any sentence that would be equally true of a plumber.

## Reusable copy blocks

Keep these in a shared content file so they stay consistent everywhere.

### Company one-liner
> Metal roofing, metal siding, and board and batten for homes and small commercial buildings across Tazewell County, Virginia.

### Company paragraph (About, footer, meta)
> Metal Master Roofing and Construction is a family-owned metal roofing and construction company working out of the Bluefield, Richlands, and Pounding Mill area of Tazewell County, Virginia. We install metal roofs, metal siding, and board and batten on homes, barns, shops, and small commercial buildings — residential work mostly, with commercial projects up to $150,000. Licensed, insured, and free estimates every time.

### Trust strip (4 items, used on most pages)
`Licensed & Insured` · `Family Owned & Local` · `Free Estimates` · `Residential & Commercial`

### CTA band (standard)
> **Free estimates, no pressure.**
> We'll come look at it, measure it right, and give you a number in writing.
> `[Call {{PHONE}}]` `[Request a Free Estimate]`

### Service area sentence
> We work throughout Tazewell County — Bluefield, Richlands, Pounding Mill, Tazewell, Cedar Bluff, Claypool Hill, and everywhere between — plus parts of Mercer County, West Virginia.

### The $150,000 line (use verbatim wherever the ceiling comes up)
> We take on residential and light commercial projects up to $150,000 — the size where the owner is still on your roof, not managing it from an office.

Never write "we can't do jobs over $150,000."

### Licensed & insured block
> Virginia contractor license `{{VA_LICENSE_NUMBER}}`. We carry general liability and workers' compensation, and we'll hand you the certificate before we set a ladder against your house. Ask any contractor for both — if they hesitate, keep calling.

*(Publish only once the license number is confirmed. Until then, use "Licensed and insured — certificate available on request.")*

## Location page opening pattern

Every location page opens with 150–200 unique words. The **structure** repeats; the **content** must not.

Pattern: `[Town] context` → `what we do there` → `why local matters here specifically` → `soft CTA`

**Example — Richlands (write ten more like this, each genuinely different):**

> Richlands sits down in the Clinch River valley, and that changes what a roof goes through. Fog settles in the low ground most mornings, the river keeps the humidity up, and older housing around town — a lot of it built during the coal years — was never put up with a fifty-year roof in mind. We install metal roofing and board and batten siding on homes throughout Richlands, from the brick ranches off Second Street to the farmhouses out past the edge of town.
>
> We're about twenty minutes up the road in Pounding Mill, so we're in Richlands constantly. If you want somebody to come look at your roof and tell you straight whether it needs replacing or just needs work, call us. The estimate's free either way.

Note what's doing the work: the river valley, the fog, the coal-era housing stock, a named street, an actual drive time. None of it transfers to Bluefield or Tazewell. That's the standard.

## FAQ answer style

Answer the question in the first sentence. Then explain. Never open with "Great question!" or "It depends."

**Good:**
> **Are metal roofs loud when it rains?**
> Not really — not on a house. The tin-roof-in-a-thunderstorm sound people remember is from panels screwed straight onto open purlins with nothing underneath, like on an old barn. On a house with solid decking and underlayment under the panels, a metal roof is about as loud as shingles. Most people don't notice a difference after the first week.

**Bad:**
> **Are metal roofs loud when it rains?**
> This is a common concern! It depends on many factors. Modern metal roofing systems have come a long way...

## Word count targets

| Page type | Words |
|---|---|
| Home | 900–1,300 |
| Flagship service (`/services/metal-roofing/`) | 1,500–2,000 |
| Standard service | 800–1,200 |
| Minor service | 600–900 |
| Priority location | 1,000–1,200 |
| Standard location | 700–1,000 |
| Blog article | 1,000–2,200 |
| About | 700–1,000 |
| Contact / Free Estimate | 300–600 |

These are targets, not quotas. **Never pad to hit a number.** A tight 700-word page outranks a bloated 1,400-word one, and Google is markedly better at detecting filler than it used to be.

## Content the owner must supply (interview, don't invent)

Schedule 45 minutes with the owner and record it. This conversation is the raw material for About, the home page, and most of the local specificity across the site.

1. How did you get into metal roofing? How long?
2. What's the job you're proudest of? Where was it?
3. What do you wish homeowners knew before they call anybody?
4. What's the biggest mistake you see on other people's roofs around here?
5. What's different about roofing in these mountains vs. anywhere flat?
6. Walk me through what happens from the first phone call to the last day.
7. What do you tell somebody who's deciding between metal and shingles?
8. What's the price range for a typical roof around here? What moves it?
9. Do you offer a workmanship warranty? How long?
10. What towns do you work in most? How far will you drive?
11. What do you *not* do?
12. Anything customers say that surprised you?

Transcribe it, then mine it. The owner's actual sentences — lightly edited — will outperform anything written from scratch, and they're the thing that makes the site sound like a person instead of a template.
