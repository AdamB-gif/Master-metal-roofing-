# 13 — Google Business Profile & Off-Site Local SEO

## Read this before building anything

**The Google Business Profile matters more for local rankings than the entire website does.**

Google Business Profile signals account for roughly a third of local pack ranking factors. Review signals account for about a fifth. On-page SEO — everything in files 02 through 10 — accounts for around 15%.

That is not an argument against building the website well. The website is what converts the traffic, it's what makes the business look real, and it feeds the entity signals the profile depends on. But if the owner does exactly one thing after launch, it should be claiming, verifying, and actively working the Google Business Profile.

`{{GBP_STATUS}}` — does a profile already exist? Is it claimed and verified? Check before creating a new one; duplicate profiles split ranking signals and are a nuisance to merge.

---

## Setup

### 1. Claim and verify
Search the business name on Google Maps. If a profile exists (auto-generated, or created earlier), claim it — do not create a duplicate. Verification is usually by postcard, phone, or video, and video verification is increasingly common for service-area businesses. Video verification typically requires showing signage, tools, vehicles with branding, and the work location — tell the owner to have branded truck lettering, equipment, and any license documentation ready.

### 2. Category — the single strongest ranking signal on the profile

**Primary category: `Roofing Contractor`.**

Not "General Contractor," not "Construction Company." The primary category is the strongest individual local ranking factor, and picking the generic one is the most common and most costly mistake contractors make.

**Secondary categories** (add all that genuinely apply):
`Metal Construction Company` · `Siding Contractor` · `Construction Company` · `Contractor`

Add only categories matching services the owner actually performs. Irrelevant categories dilute relevance.

### 3. Service-area business configuration

The owner is supplying an address later. For a contractor who travels to customers:

- **Hide the address** and configure the profile as a service-area business. If the address is a home, this is also the right privacy call.
- **Set service areas** to the actual towns served — up to 20 areas. Prioritize: Tazewell County VA, Bluefield VA, Richlands, Pounding Mill, Tazewell, Cedar Bluff, North Tazewell, Claypool Hill, Raven, Bluefield WV, Princeton WV.
- Google generally shows service-area businesses to searchers within the declared area. Don't inflate it — a service area covering half of Virginia dilutes relevance everywhere and produces calls the owner won't drive to.

### 4. Complete every field

Google explicitly favors completed profiles. Fill in all of it:

- **Business name:** `Metal Master Roofing and Construction` — exactly matching the website and schema. **Do not stuff keywords into the name** ("Metal Master Roofing - Best Metal Roofing Bluefield VA"). It's a guideline violation, competitors report it, and it can get the profile suspended.
- **Description** (750 chars): natural language, keywords worked in honestly. Adapt the company paragraph from `10-content-copy-guide.md`.
- **Services:** list each one individually with its own description — metal roof installation, standing seam, exposed fastener, board and batten metal siding, metal siding, roof repair, roof replacement, commercial metal roofing, pole barns. Each service entry is an additional relevance signal.
- **Attributes:** family-owned, veteran-owned (if applicable), free estimates, online estimates, appointment required, languages.
- **Hours:** accurate, including holidays. Wrong hours produce bad experiences that show up in reviews.
- **Phone:** the same number as the website. Byte-identical.
- **Website:** the home page URL.
- **Opening date:** `{{YEAR_ESTABLISHED}}`.
- **Logo and cover photo:** the same logo used for the favicon. Consistency across the website, GBP, and Facebook is what lets Google resolve all three into one entity.

## Ongoing work (this is where local rankings are actually won)

The profile is not a set-and-forget listing. Photo recency, post frequency, review velocity, and response speed all feed live into ranking.

### Photos — weekly

Upload 2–5 job photos every week. Recency is a live signal, and photos are the most-viewed element of any contractor profile.

**Enable location tagging on the phone camera** before shooting job photos. Geo-tagged photos tell Google where the business actually operates, reinforcing the declared service area. Then strip the EXIF before publishing the same photos to the website (see `09-assets-and-images.md`) — GBP and the website have different privacy considerations.

### Posts — weekly

Short updates, 150–300 words, each with a photo and a call-to-action button:
- Completed job in a named town
- Seasonal note ("before winter, check your fastener washers")
- A common question, answered
- A new color or product

Link posts to relevant website pages. This is a free, direct channel to the profile that most local competitors ignore entirely.

### Reviews — the highest-leverage ongoing activity

**Velocity beats volume.** A steady stream of new reviews outranks a larger pile of old ones. Ten reviews over ten months beats forty from three years ago.

**Process for the owner:**
1. Ask **every** satisfied customer, at job completion, in person. This is the whole thing — most contractors simply never ask.
2. Send the direct review link by text the same day, while the finished roof is still the most interesting thing in their life. Get the short link from GBP: Share review form.
3. Print a small card with a QR code to the review link. Hand it over with the invoice.
4. **Respond to every review within 24–48 hours**, positive and negative. Response speed is a ranking signal and responses are visible to every future customer reading the profile.
5. Respond to negative reviews calmly, factually, and briefly — offer to resolve it offline. Future customers read the response more carefully than the complaint. A measured reply to an unfair review builds more trust than a wall of five stars.

**Never** buy reviews, offer discounts in exchange for reviews, or write reviews from staff accounts. Google detects review-gating and incentivization, and the penalties range from review removal to profile suspension. In a county this size it's also a reputational risk that isn't worth taking.

**Target:** 2–4 new reviews per month, sustained. Within a year that's a profile no thin national competitor can match.

### Q&A

Google lets anyone ask a question on the profile — and the owner can seed questions and answer them. Pre-load the ten most common questions with good answers. Unanswered questions get answered by strangers, sometimes wrongly.

### Messaging

Enable **only if it will be monitored.** An unanswered messaging channel is worse than a disabled one — response time is visible and tracked.

## Citations and directories

Consistent NAP (Name, Address, Phone) across the web. Even for a service-area business without a published address, name and phone must match exactly everywhere.

**Priority listings, in order:**
1. Google Business Profile
2. Facebook (already exists — update it to match the website exactly, add the website link, use the same logo)
3. Apple Business Connect (Apple Maps — often overlooked, no competition, and it's what iPhone users get by default)
4. Bing Places
5. Yelp
6. Better Business Bureau (only if the owner wants to pay for accreditation — an unclaimed BBB listing is neutral)
7. Angi / HomeAdvisor — optional, and be aware these sell shared leads to competitors
8. Nextdoor — genuinely valuable in small communities; neighbor recommendations carry real weight here
9. Virginia DPOR license lookup — already a public record; make sure the business name there matches

**Local citations worth pursuing:**
- Tazewell County Chamber of Commerce
- Local business associations in Richlands, Bluefield, Tazewell
- Sponsorships — youth sports, fire department, county fair. These produce genuine local backlinks *and* the kind of word-of-mouth that matters more than any of it in a county of 40,000 people.

**Do not** buy bulk citation packages or use automated directory-submission services. Low-quality directory spam does nothing at best.

## Realistic timeline

| Timeframe | Expect |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | Site live, GBP claimed, Search Console verified, sitemap submitted |
| Weeks 2–4 | Pages indexed. Favicon appears. Branded search ("Metal Master Roofing") ranks #1. |
| Months 2–3 | Long-tail blog and location pages start showing impressions. First local pack appearances in the home town. |
| Months 4–6 | Competitive local pack positions in priority towns, assuming reviews are accumulating. Meaningful organic traffic. |
| Months 6–12 | Established local authority. The cost article and comparison articles become steady lead sources. |

**Local SEO is slow and then it isn't.** The first two months look like nothing is happening. Don't let the owner conclude the site failed at week six — set this expectation before launch, in writing.

The two things that most compress this timeline: **review velocity** and **real local content**. Both are largely in the owner's hands, not the developer's.
