# Page Specs — Blog / Content Hub

## Why the blog exists

Not for its own sake. Three specific jobs:

1. **Capture research-stage searches.** Someone googling "how much does a metal roof cost in Virginia" is 2–8 weeks from hiring somebody. Get to them first.
2. **Build topical authority.** A site with ten substantive articles on metal roofing is more credible to Google on the topic "metal roofing" than a site with none. That authority lifts the service and location pages.
3. **Get cited by AI answers.** Search summaries and AI assistants pull from pages that answer questions directly and plainly. Articles structured as clear answers to real questions get quoted; marketing copy never does.

**Quality over cadence.** Ten strong articles beat forty thin ones. Do not publish filler.

---

## Universal article template

```
1. Breadcrumb              Home › Blog › [Title]
2. H1                      The question, or a direct promise
3. Byline + date           Author: {{OWNER_NAME}}, Metal Master Roofing. Real dates.
4. Quick answer            40–80 words in a styled box, immediately under the H1.
                           This wins featured snippets and AI citations. Never skip it.
5. Table of contents       Jump links, for articles over 1,200 words
6. Body                    H2/H3 sections, tables, real photos
7. Local angle             At least one section specific to southwest Virginia
8. Related articles        2–3 internal links
9. CTA band                Call + Free Estimate
```

**Rules for every article:**
- Minimum 1,000 words. Target 1,400–2,000 for the comparison and cost pieces.
- Minimum 2 internal links to service pages, 1 to a location page — in body text, descriptive anchors.
- Minimum 1 real photo from the company's own work. No stock roofs.
- Written from a contractor's chair, not a copywriter's. Include the thing only someone who has done the work would say.
- Real publish and update dates in `Article` schema. Never fake freshness by rewriting dates without touching content.
- **Say the true thing even when it doesn't sell.** "A metal roof isn't the right answer if you're selling the house in two years" builds more trust — and converts better — than pretending metal is always right.

## Article briefs

### 1. `/blog/metal-roof-cost-southwest-virginia/` ★ highest-value article
**H1:** `How Much Does a Metal Roof Cost in Southwest Virginia?`
**Words:** 1,800–2,200

The article most likely to produce phone calls. Nobody local answers this question honestly, which is exactly the opening.

Sections: the short answer (real per-square-foot range, `{{PRICE_LOW}}`–`{{PRICE_HIGH}}` — get the owner's real numbers) · what changes the price (roof size, pitch, complexity, valleys, dormers, penetrations, panel type, gauge, color, tear-off vs. over-shingles, decking condition, access) · standing seam vs. exposed fastener cost table · cost vs. asphalt over 40 years, with the arithmetic shown · what a cheap bid usually means (thin gauge, no underlayment, wrong fasteners, no insurance) · how to read an estimate · financing `{{FINANCING}}`.

**Include an actual worked example.** "A 1,800 sq ft simple gable ranch in Richlands with a 6/12 pitch, going with 29-gauge exposed fastener over existing shingles: roughly `{{EXAMPLE_PRICE}}`." Concrete beats ranges.

Links: metal roofing, standing seam, exposed fastener, replacement, free estimate, 2 town pages.

### 2. `/blog/metal-roof-vs-shingles/`
**H1:** `Metal Roof vs. Shingles: Which Is Right for a Virginia Home?`
**Words:** 1,500–1,800

Full comparison table: upfront cost, lifespan, maintenance, weight, snow/ice, wind rating, fire, energy, resale, noise, appearance, insurance. Then: **when shingles are actually the better call** — short remaining ownership, tight budget, complex roof geometry, HOA restrictions. That honesty is the whole credibility of the piece. Cover the 40-year math and the Appalachian weather case.

### 3. `/blog/standing-seam-vs-exposed-fastener/`
**H1:** `Standing Seam vs. Exposed Fastener: Which Metal Roof Should You Get?`
**Words:** 1,400–1,700

How each is fastened and why it matters. Cost delta (exposed fastener roughly half the installed cost). Lifespan. Maintenance reality: exposed fastener screws back out, washers degrade, every screw hole is an eventual leak point — plan on inspection and eventual re-screw. Standing seam's concealed clips allow thermal movement and eliminate surface penetrations. Show the 50-year total-cost math where standing seam typically wins by a wide margin. Then be practical: on a barn or a shop, exposed fastener is often the right call and standing seam is overkill.

### 5. `/blog/how-long-does-a-metal-roof-last/`
**H1:** `How Long Does a Metal Roof Last?`
**Words:** 1,000–1,300

Realistic lifespan by type (standing seam 40–70 years; exposed fastener 20–40 with maintenance). What actually shortens it: fastener failure, coastal or industrial exposure, poor installation, incompatible metals in contact, standing debris and trapped moisture. What extends it: proper underlayment, correct fastener spec, keeping valleys clear. Warranty types explained — paint vs. substrate vs. workmanship are three different things and homeowners conflate them constantly.

### 6. `/blog/metal-roof-colors-guide/`
**H1:** `Choosing a Metal Roof Color: What Works in Southwest Virginia`
**Words:** 1,000–1,300

Popular colors and what they do on a real house. Dark colors on mountain homes: shows less dirt, sheds snow marginally faster, runs hotter. Light colors: cooler attic, shows pollen and dust. ### 7. `/blog/can-you-put-a-metal-roof-over-shingles/`
**H1:** `Can You Put a Metal Roof Over Shingles?`
**Words:** 1,000–1,200

Usually yes, with conditions. When it works: sound decking, one existing layer, code and manufacturer allowance. When it doesn't: multiple layers, rotted decking, structural load concerns, wavy substrate telegraphing through panels. Furring strips and underlayment. Cost savings from skipping tear-off. Local code `{{LOCAL_CODE}}` — verify Tazewell County requirements before publishing specifics. The honest downside: you can't see what's under there, and problems get sealed in.

### 9. `/blog/metal-roofs-snow-ice-appalachian-winters/`
**H1:** `How Metal Roofs Handle Snow and Ice in the Virginia Mountains`
**Words:** 1,200–1,400

Strongly regional, almost no competition. Snow shedding on smooth panels and why that's mostly good and occasionally a hazard. Snow guards over doorways, walkways, and driveways. ### 10. `/blog/how-to-choose-a-roofing-contractor-in-tazewell-county/`
**H1:** `How to Choose a Roofing Contractor in Tazewell County`
**Words:** 1,200–1,500

Bottom-funnel. Reads as a public service, functions as a sales page — because a genuinely honest checklist happens to describe this company.

Cover: verify the Virginia license through DPOR and check the class against the job size (Class C up to $29,999, Class B $30,000–$149,999, Class A above that — hiring outside a contractor's class is a real problem for the homeowner too). Ask for a certificate of insurance from the carrier, not a photocopy. Get everything in writing. Understand deposit norms and never pay in full up front. Beware storm-chasers and out-of-area crews with magnetic door signs. Read reviews for patterns, not scores. Ask to see local work. Warranty in writing. Red flags: door-knocking after storms, pressure to sign today, cash-only, no written estimate, an offer to "handle your deductible" — that last one is insurance fraud and it makes the homeowner a participant.

Close with a soft, non-pushy CTA.
